Nobody wakes up at 36 and finds their muscle has gone. It leaves slowly, a little each year, and most people only notice when the shopping bags start feeling heavier than they used to.

## The slow leak

Muscle mass drops by roughly 3 to 8% per decade after 30, and the rate picks up after 60.<sup class="cite"><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> Over one decade that's easy to miss. Over three it isn't.

Muscle still responds to training at any age, which makes this one of the more fixable parts of getting older.

## Why muscle matters more than it looks

Muscle gets you up the stairs and off the floor. It carries the suitcase, and it gets you through a weekend of DIY without needing a lie down afterwards.

A 2022 meta analysis in the British Journal of Sports Medicine pooled 16 cohort studies. People who did 30 to 60 minutes of muscle strengthening a week had a 10 to 20% lower risk of death from all causes, cardiovascular disease and cancer. Combining strength work with aerobic exercise was linked to around a 40% lower risk of death from all causes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#ref-3">[3]</a></sup>

These were observational studies, so they show an association rather than proof. The biggest differences showed up at 30 to 60 minutes a week, which is less than most people assume.

## What the UK guidelines say

The UK Chief Medical Officers recommend strength work on at least two days a week. That sits on top of at least 150 minutes of moderate activity or 75 minutes of vigorous activity.<sup class="cite"><a href="#ref-2">[2]</a></sup> Strength has its own line in the guidance, separate from the cardio target.

## How much you actually need

Two or three full body sessions a week covers most people. In practice that's a couple of lunch breaks.

Build each session around big movements: a squat or leg press, a hinge such as a deadlift, a push, a pull and some core work. Bodyweight exercises count, especially at the start.

Make it a little harder as the weeks go on. An extra rep or a bit more weight is enough. This is progressive overload, and it's what keeps your body adapting.

Finish most sets with a couple of reps left in the tank. If you're coming back after years away, start lighter than your ego would like.

## Recovery is where the work pays off

The session gives your body a reason to adapt. The adapting happens afterwards, mostly while you sleep, so sleep comes first.

Protein matters too. A meta analysis found muscle gains levelled off at around 1.6g of protein per kg of bodyweight per day.<sup class="cite"><a href="#ref-6">[6]</a></sup> Rest days help, and walking or easy Zone 2 work in between still counts as rest. Skimp on recovery and Thursday's session tends to quietly disappear from the diary.

Two micronutrients have authorised claims linked to muscle:<sup class="cite"><a href="#ref-4">[4]</a></sup>

- ✓ Magnesium contributes to normal muscle function.
- ✓ Magnesium contributes to a reduction of tiredness and fatigue.
- ✓ Vitamin D contributes to the maintenance of normal muscle function.

## Where creatine comes in

Creatine is one of the most researched supplements for people who train.<sup class="cite"><a href="#ref-4">[4]</a></sup>

- ✓ Creatine increases physical performance in successive bursts of short-term, high intensity exercise.

The beneficial effect is obtained with a daily intake of 3g of creatine. This claim is for adults performing high intensity exercise.

There is also an authorised claim for people over 55:<sup class="cite"><a href="#ref-4">[4]</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#ref-5">[5]</a></sup>

- ✓ Daily creatine consumption can enhance the effect of resistance training on muscle strength in adults over the age of 55.

This claim is for adults over the age of 55 who do regular resistance training. The beneficial effect is obtained with a daily intake of 3g of creatine in conjunction with resistance training that increases the workload over time, performed at least three times per week for several weeks at an intensity of at least 65 to 75% of one repetition maximum.

Creatine works alongside the training, so the sessions still have to happen.

Two or three sessions a week, a little harder over time, decent sleep and enough protein. Muscle is much easier to keep than to win back, so the best time to start is before you need to.
