Magnesium: The Mineral With a Very Long To Do List

Magnesium is involved in more than 300 enzyme systems in the body. What it does, how much adults need, why the form matters and how it fits into one daily scoop.

Magnesium rarely gets the attention vitamin D does, which is a little unfair given how much work it does.

A mineral with hundreds of jobs

Magnesium is a cofactor in more than 300 enzyme systems in the body. These regulate reactions including protein synthesis, muscle and nerve function, blood glucose control and blood pressure regulation. It's also needed for energy production and for making DNA and RNA.[1]

The UK authorised claims cover a fair bit of that ground:[2]

  • ✓ Magnesium contributes to a reduction of tiredness and fatigue.
  • ✓ Magnesium contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism.
  • ✓ Magnesium contributes to normal functioning of the nervous system.
  • ✓ Magnesium contributes to normal muscle function.
  • ✓ Magnesium contributes to normal psychological function.

How much you need

The NHS says adults aged 19 to 64 need 300mg of magnesium a day for men and 270mg a day for women.[3] Good food sources include spinach, nuts and wholemeal bread.

If you take a supplement, the NHS says having 400mg or less a day from supplements is unlikely to cause any harm.[3] Scōp contains 400mg per serving, so if you're already taking a separate magnesium supplement, you won't need both.

Why the form matters

Magnesium comes in lots of forms, and the label should tell you which one you're getting. Scōp uses magnesium bisglycinate, where magnesium is bound to the amino acid glycine. It's a form that's generally well tolerated.

Why people fall short

Plenty of everyday diets lean on refined foods, and processing strips out parts of grains that contain magnesium. A busy routine doesn't help either. Spinach and nuts are easy to forget about when lunch is a meal deal eaten at your desk.

That's the case for a steady daily intake rather than an occasional good week.

One less thing to think about

A separate magnesium tablet is one more thing to buy, remember and reorder. In Scōp it's part of the same scoop as your creatine, vitamin D3 and K2 and all eight B vitamins, so it gets taken whenever the rest does.
