Methylated B Vitamins: What the Label Actually Means

What “methylated” and “active” mean on a B vitamin label, why the form matters and exactly which B vitamins are in every scoop of Scōp.

There are eight B vitamins: thiamin (B1), riboflavin (B2), niacin (B3), pantothenic acid (B5), vitamin B6, biotin (B7), folate (B9) and vitamin B12. They don't give you energy themselves. They work as coenzymes, the helper molecules your enzymes need to turn food into energy your cells can use.

Eight vitamins with overlapping jobs

Seven of the eight have the same authorised claim:[2]

  • ✓ Thiamine contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism.
  • ✓ Riboflavin contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism.
  • ✓ Niacin contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism.
  • ✓ Pantothenic acid contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism.
  • ✓ Vitamin B6 contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism.
  • ✓ Biotin contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism.
  • ✓ Vitamin B12 contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism.

Six of them also have this one:[2]

  • ✓ Riboflavin contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue.
  • ✓ Niacin contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue.
  • ✓ Pantothenic acid contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue.
  • ✓ Vitamin B6 contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue.
  • ✓ Folate contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue.
  • ✓ Vitamin B12 contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue.

They also depend on each other. Riboflavin is needed to turn vitamin B6 into its active form.[3] Folate, vitamin B6 and vitamin B12 work in the same pathway:[2]

  • ✓ Folate contributes to normal homocysteine metabolism.
  • ✓ Vitamin B6 contributes to normal homocysteine metabolism.
  • ✓ Vitamin B12 contributes to normal homocysteine metabolism.

A 2016 review made the case for looking at all eight together rather than one or two in isolation.[1]

What "methylated" means

A methyl group is a small cluster of one carbon atom and three hydrogen atoms. On a label, "methylated" means the vitamin already carries one. In Scōp that applies to two vitamins: folate as L-methylfolate and vitamin B12 as methylcobalamin.

Two more come in their active coenzyme forms, the versions your body actually puts to work. Riboflavin is supplied as riboflavin-5′-phosphate (also called FMN). Vitamin B6 is supplied as pyridoxal 5′-phosphate (P-5-P), whereas most supplements use pyridoxine hydrochloride.[6]

Thiamin, niacin (as nicotinamide), pantothenic acid and biotin complete the set.

Folate and why the form matters

Folic acid, the form in most supplements and fortified foods, has to be converted by an enzyme called dihydrofolate reductase before your body can use it. Research on human liver found this enzyme works slowly and varies a lot from person to person.[7]

L-methylfolate is already in the form that circulates in your body. EFSA has assessed calcium L-methylfolate, the form used in Scōp, as a bioavailable source of folate.[8]

  • ✓ Folate contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue.
  • ✓ Folate contributes to normal psychological function.

If you're pregnant or trying for a baby, the NHS recommends a 400 µg folic acid supplement every day until 12 weeks of pregnancy.[9] Speak to your GP or midwife about what's right for you.

Vitamin B12 as methylcobalamin

Cyanocobalamin is the most common synthetic form of B12, and your body converts it into methylcobalamin and adenosylcobalamin.[5] Scōp supplies methylcobalamin directly.

  • ✓ Vitamin B12 contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism.
  • ✓ Vitamin B12 contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue.
  • ✓ Vitamin B12 contributes to normal functioning of the nervous system.

Why 900 µg?

Your body absorbs B12 mainly through a carrier protein that can only handle small amounts at a time. As the dose goes up, the share you absorb goes down.[10] That's why B12 supplements typically show a high % NRV. No upper safety limit has been set for vitamin B12.[11]

Especially relevant for vegetarians

B12 occurs naturally only in animal foods.[10] That makes it particularly relevant for vegetarians and vegans. Older adults and people taking certain medicines, such as metformin or acid reducing medicines, are also more likely to run low.[10]

Scōp is suitable for vegetarians and contains 900 µg of B12 per serving. If you're worried about your levels, your GP can arrange a blood test.

Understanding the daily amounts

Vitamin B6: 10mg per serving, within the EFSA adult upper limit of 12mg a day.[12] Don't combine Scōp with other vitamin B6 supplements.

Folate: 400 µg per serving, within the 1,000 µg upper limit.[11]

Niacin: 48mg of nicotinamide per serving, well within the 900mg upper limit for nicotinamide.[11] Nicotinamide is a different form from nicotinic acid, the form that can cause skin flushes at high doses.[9]

Biotin can interfere with some laboratory tests. If you're having blood tests, tell your doctor you take it.[13]

The bottom line

The form of a B vitamin decides how much work your body has to do before it can use it. Scōp covers all eight, with folate and B12 in methylated forms and B2 and B6 in their active coenzyme forms.

Common questions

What does "methylated" mean on a B vitamin label?

It means the vitamin already carries a methyl group, as with L-methylfolate and methylcobalamin. Scōp also uses the active coenzyme forms of B2 and B6.

What is the difference between L-methylfolate and folic acid?

Folic acid has to be converted by an enzyme before your body can use it. L-methylfolate is already in the circulating form, and EFSA has assessed calcium L-methylfolate as a bioavailable source of folate. If you're pregnant or trying for a baby, follow NHS guidance on folic acid.

What is methylcobalamin?

It's one of the two coenzyme forms of vitamin B12 your body uses. Vitamin B12 contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism. Vitamin B12 contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue. Vitamin B12 contributes to normal functioning of the nervous system.

Why does Scōp contain 900 µg of vitamin B12?

B12 is absorbed through a carrier protein with limited capacity, so a smaller share of a larger dose is absorbed. No upper safety limit has been set for B12.

Is the vitamin B6 in Scōp within safe limits?

Yes. Scōp contains 10mg per serving, within the EFSA adult upper limit of 12mg a day. Don't combine it with other vitamin B6 supplements.

Is Scōp a good choice for vegetarians?

Scōp is suitable for vegetarians, and because B12 occurs naturally only in animal foods, the 900 µg per serving is particularly relevant.
